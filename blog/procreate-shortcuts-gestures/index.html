15 Essential Procreate Shortcuts & Gestures Every Beginner Should Know

By the DigiKitPro studio · Published 20 September 2026

Procreate has one of the fastest learning curves of any painting app, and most of that speed is hidden in its gestures. The buttons you see on screen are the slow path: every tap is a round trip between your hand and the toolbar, and every round trip breaks your drawing flow.

Gestures are the fast path. They are built into the canvas itself, they work with your hand already where it needs to be, and once they become muscle memory they simply stop existing as separate actions. This guide lists the fifteen that matter most, grouped by how often you will actually use them, and it ends with a fifteen-minute drill to get them into your fingers.

Nothing here requires an update, an accessory, or a purchase. If you already use Procreate, you are probably using five of these. The other ten are where the time is.

Why gestures beat buttons

There are three reasons gestures change how Procreate feels after a few weeks of practice.

  • Your hand stays on the canvas. A two-finger tap to undo happens in the same place your drawing hand is resting. The undo button in the top bar requires lifting your hand, aiming, and tapping — three small movements that add up to minutes per session.
  • Gestures are continuous. A pinch zoom follows your fingers in real time. A slider you drag in the interface requires locating the slider first. For anything you adjust repeatedly, continuous control is faster by a lot.
  • Undo history is deep. Procreate remembers up to 250 steps, and the two-finger tap gives you instant access to the whole stack. Beginners who avoid undo because it feels slow end up painting over mistakes instead of removing them.

The pattern that separates fast artists from slow ones is simple: gestures for anything you do more than once a day, buttons for everything else.

The four gestures you will use every single day

Learn these first and practise them until they are boring. They cover undo, redo, and rapid stepping — the actions that account for the majority of gesture use in any session.

1. Two-finger tap — Undo

Tap the canvas with two fingers, together or apart, to undo your last action. A small notification at the top of the screen tells you what was undone, which is useful for the first week while you learn what each step affected.

Two details worth knowing: undo history is up to 250 steps, and the history clears the moment you return to the Gallery or quit Procreate — that is when the work is saved permanently. So if you have done something risky, a quick trip to the Gallery and back locks it in.

2. Three-finger tap — Redo

Tap with three fingers to bring back a step you just undid. It is the mirror of undo, and it is the gesture that makes experimenting painless: you can take a risky stroke, undo it, and keep the confidence that you can always come back.

3. Two-finger tap and hold — Rapid undo

Tap with two fingers and keep them down. After a short delay, Procreate starts stepping backwards through your history quickly. Lift your fingers to stop. This is the fastest way to return to a state you liked ten minutes ago without counting taps.

If the delay before rapid undo starts feels too short or too long, you can adjust it in Actions → Prefs under Rapid Undo Delay.

4. Three-finger tap and hold — Rapid redo

The same idea in the other direction: hold three fingers down and Procreate rapidly steps forward through the steps you undid. Release to stop.

Navigating the canvas without touching the toolbar

Zoom, fit, and rotate are the navigation trio. Once these are automatic, you stop losing time repositioning your view.

5. Pinch to zoom

Two fingers on the canvas, pinch apart to zoom in, pinch together to zoom out. This is the gesture for checking fine detail — the catchlight in an eye, a line weight, a texture. Pinch zoom at 100% or less for big-picture work, deep zoom for detail.

6. Quick pinch — Fit to screen

A very fast pinch motion — quicker than a normal pinch — snaps the whole canvas to fit the screen instantly. It is the fastest way to answer the question how does the whole piece look?

The reverse quick pinch (a fast pinch apart after the canvas is fitted) returns you to the exact view you had before. That round trip — quick pinch out, look, quick pinch back — should become a single habit: check the whole piece, then return without losing your position.

7. Pinch-twist — Rotate the canvas

Start a pinch and twist your fingers in a circle. The canvas rotates to any angle, which is genuinely useful: a portrait that is easier to see with the chin tilted, a landscape you want to level without moving your iPad. You can rotate back the same way, and there is a rotate control in the top bar if you ever prefer the button.

One practical note: with an Apple Pencil in your hand, one-finger drags move the canvas around the screen. When you are painting with your fingertip instead, the canvas moves with a two-finger tap and drag, because a one-finger drag is painting in that mode.

Editing gestures most artists never try

These three save real time on the unglamorous parts of a session — clearing, copying, and focusing.

8. Three-finger scrub — Clear a layer

Move three fingers side to side in a scrubbing motion across the canvas and the current layer is cleared instantly. No menu, no confirmation dialog. And because it is a normal action, a two-finger tap brings the layer straight back. This is the cleanest way to retry a whole element — a background, a hair mass, a shadow pass — without touching the layer options.

9. Three-finger swipe down — Cut, Copy, Paste menu

Swipe three fingers down the screen and a floating menu appears with Cut, Copy, Copy All, Duplicate, Cut and Paste, and Paste. It is the fastest route to duplicating an element (a leaf, a cloud, a character you want to repeat) or moving part of a layer to a new one. Most artists find this within their first week and then never use the interface buttons again.

10. Four-finger tap — Full screen

Tap four fingers on the screen and the entire interface slides away, leaving nothing but your canvas. Tap four fingers again (or tap the small indicator in the corner) to bring it back. It sounds minor, but for concentration work — long shading passes, detailed line work — the absence of the toolbar changes how much you notice of your own process.

QuickShape: the accuracy cheat code

11. Draw and hold

Draw a line, a circle, or any rough shape — and before you lift your finger, hold there. The stroke snaps to the straightest line or the most perfect shape Procreate can detect. While you are holding, tap with a second finger to cycle to the exact version you want.

QuickShape is the difference between a wobbly horizon and a dead-straight one, and it works with every brush. It is also quietly essential for beginners: it makes straight lines available without a ruler layer, and it trains your eye on where the true line sits relative to your attempt.

Layer gestures that save real time

Procreate's Layers panel has its own gesture set, and this is where the workflow differences really show up. All of these happen in the panel itself.

12. Two-finger tap a layer — Opacity

Two-finger tap a layer in the Layers panel and a thin opacity bar appears at the top of the canvas. Drag left to make the layer more transparent, drag right to make it more solid. If you have an active selection when you do this, the opacity change applies only inside the selection — a genuinely useful trick for fading out part of a layer.

13. Two-finger swipe right — Alpha Lock

Swipe two fingers from left to right on a layer and Alpha Lock engages: the layer's transparent pixels are locked, so everything you paint from then on stays inside the existing shapes. It is the fastest way to add shading or texture to a base layer without bleeding outside the edges. Our layers explained guide covers Alpha Lock versus Clipping Mask in detail, including when each one is the better tool.

14. Two-finger tap and hold a layer — Select its contents

Two-finger tap and hold on a layer's thumbnail and Procreate selects everything drawn on that layer — the painted pixels only, not the transparency. From there you can move, duplicate, feather, transform, or clear the selection. It is the fastest path from "I want that thing on this layer" to having it in your hand.

Bonus: Pinch two layers together — Merge

In the Layers panel, pinch two layers together and they merge into one (any layers between them merge as well). It is faster than the merge button and useful for flattening a group once you know it is finished. Just remember that merging is destructive — the layers become one — so keep the habit of merging only when you are done with an element.

Beyond the basics: hover, Pencil double-tap, and custom gestures

If you have an Apple Pencil with hover support (2nd generation Pencil with a compatible iPad Pro, on iPadOS 16.1 or newer), Procreate adds two more gestures that work while the pencil hovers just above the canvas:

  • Pinch while hovering changes your brush size — pinch in to shrink, pinch out to grow — without touching the screen.
  • Slide left or right while hovering changes your brush opacity.

Both are worth switching on in Actions → Prefs → Gesture Controls, and both work beautifully with the Brush Cursor preference: you can match the size or opacity of an existing stroke by eye before you commit to the next one.

The Apple Pencil double-tap is the other hidden control: double-tap the barrel of the pencil mid-stroke to switch to eraser, open the colour wheel, or undo — you choose the action in Actions → Prefs → Gesture Controls. Setting it to Eraser is the single most recommended configuration for line work, because it turns a correction into a thumb-free action.

Finally, the Gesture Controls panel (Actions → Prefs → Gesture Controls) is where all of these live. You can turn gestures off, add alternative triggers, set hold delays, and enable the Single Touch Gestures Companion — a floating panel with on-screen Undo, Redo, Zoom, Move, and Fit Canvas buttons — which is the right answer for artists who find multi-finger gestures unreliable.

A fifteen-minute muscle-memory drill

Gestures do not become automatic by reading about them. Open a blank canvas — the size does not matter for this — and do the following loop until you have completed it three times without thinking:

  1. Draw five rough lines. Hold the last one and let QuickShape snap it.
  2. Two-finger tap to undo. Three-finger tap to redo. Tap-hold with two fingers to rapid-undo back to the start.
  3. Pinch to zoom in on a line, then quick pinch to fit, then quick pinch back.
  4. Make a new layer, fill it with a few marks, two-finger tap the layer and drag opacity down to 40%, back to 100%.
  5. Three-finger scrub to clear the layer. Two-finger tap to bring it back.
  6. Four-finger tap into full screen, draw one mark, four-finger tap back.

Three loops takes about fifteen minutes. Do it once a day for a week and the gestures stop being gestures — they just become how you use the app.
